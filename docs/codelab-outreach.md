# CodeLab Outreach

## Project Sourcing Positioning

Use this framing when reaching out to strong network leads for CodeLab projects:

> I am trying to source a few technically serious projects for CodeLab this year. Your background seemed unusually relevant, and I am trying to understand where a student team could build something genuinely useful rather than another generic dashboard. Would you be open to a short conversation, or point me to the right person?

## Why It Works

- It asks for discovery, not a commitment.
- It signals taste: serious technical work, not generic student projects.
- It respects their time and gives them an easy referral path.
- It works for sponsors, routers, scouts, and advisors.

## Practice Outreach Workflow

Use `Practice Outreach` before messaging reputation-sensitive contacts. This lane is for low-risk reps where silence or rejection is cheap, but the person still has plausible technical project value.

Relationship risk labels:

- `Cold Practice`: strangers or loose contacts where outreach has low social cost.
- `Warm Light`: reachable people with mild connection strength, but no family or community sensitivity.
- `Warm Sensitive`: people tied to family, respected community relationships, or prior in-person small talk. Research first, then ask for context if needed.
- `Big Ask Later`: major prestige or high-leverage contacts to save until the pitch is proven.
- `Avoid / Need Context`: awkward, unclear, or potentially damaging outreach paths.

Do not use `Warm Sensitive`, `Big Ask Later`, or `Avoid / Need Context` as practice reps. Mark them with relationship context and source-backed research before any message.

Research dossiers should be source-backed. Every factual claim should include a URL in `Research Source URLs` and enough note context in `Research Dossier` to support a conversation hook.
